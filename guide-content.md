## Exported Scorecard

  - **eyebrow** Sharing
  - **h1** Exported scorecard
  - **lead** You cannot stand at four courts at once. Hand a scorecard to whoever is standing at the other three.
  - **badge** How it works
    - **label** Export the card
      - **cap** TournaQ turns the game — or a whole court — into a QR code.
    - **label** They scan it
      - **cap** On their own phone. No account, no install of your tournament, no connection needed.
    - **label** They score the game
      - **cap** A full scorecard on their side, with the right teams and format already on it.
    - **label** You collect the result
      - **cap** Scan it back and the result drops into your tournament.
  - **title** Paperless and offline
    - **body** The whole exchange is two QR codes. Nothing goes through a server, so it works on a beach with no signal exactly as well as it does indoors.
  - **title** Draft note · direction of the arrow
    - **body** Your Principle diagram shows lines running <b>both ways</b> between Tournament and Exported Scorecard, which raised a question in the source notes. The draft treats that as correct and deliberate: the card goes out, the result comes back. It is a round trip, not a one-way export.

## Swiss System

  - **eyebrow** Team competition
  - **h1** Swiss System
  - **lead** Paired each round against someone on the same score as you. No eliminations, a full schedule for everybody, and the field sorts itself.
  - **badge** How it runs
    - **label** Round 1 drawn
      - **cap** Pairings from the seeding, or at random.
    - **label** Then: like against like
      - **cap** Every following round pairs teams by current score, so games stay close.
    - **label** As many rounds as you want
      - **cap** Take the suggested number of games, or set your own.
    - **label** Standings decide
      - **cap** No bracket, no knockout — the table at the end is the result.
  - **title** Best when the field is large
    - **body** Swiss gets a fair ranking out of many teams in far fewer games than a full round robin would take.

## Social Scramble

  - **eyebrow** Classic mode
  - **h1** Social Scramble
  - **lead** A timed, rotating mixer where teams are redrawn every round. Nobody stays partnered for long — the whole point is to play with and against as many different people as possible.
  - **badge** How a round works
    - **label** Teams are drawn
      - **cap** Randomly, at the start of every round.
    - **label** All courts play at once
      - **cap** For the set match duration.
    - **label** Time, then a short break
      - **cap** Before the next round is drawn.
    - **label** Wins accumulate
      - **cap** Cumulative across all rounds — the standings are yours, not your team’s.
  - **title** Fair by design
    - **body** TournaQ schedules every player into the maximum number of rounds while keeping wait times as short as possible. When not everyone fits on court, sitting-out rotations are balanced so no player waits longer than others.
  - **title** Good for
    - **body** Beach sessions, open days, and any group that wants competitive play without the pressure of a fixed bracket.

## Scramble King

  - **eyebrow** Queue mode
  - **h1** Scramble King
  - **lead** King of the court, played as a scramble. You get a partner for the round, hold the court as long as you can win, and then the whole field reshuffles.
  - **badge** How it works
    - **label** Partners drawn for the round
      - **cap** Your partner is fixed for the round — and different in the next one.
    - **label** Hold the court
      - **cap** Win and you stay. Lose and you go back to the queue.
    - **label** The round ends on the clock
      - **cap** Then the field is scrambled again.
    - **label** Individual standings
      - **cap** Every point and every game is credited to you, not to the pairing.
  - **title** Referees rotate independently
    - **body** Whoever is off court can be suggested as referee, drawn fairly and independently of the partner rotation — it does not depend on there being an odd player.
  - **title** Draft note · which wording is right?
    - **body** The source diagrams disagree: one says “<b>a new partner every round</b>”, the other “<b>a fixed partner for 1 round</b>”. The draft assumes these describe the same thing from two sides — <b>fixed within the round, new in the next</b> — and words it that way. Confirm before this ships.


## King of the Court

  - **eyebrow** Queue mode
  - **h1** King of the Court
  - **lead** An individual competition across as many courts as you have. Every player queues for themselves — when your turn comes you are paired with whoever is next, so your partner changes constantly and every point, game and ranking point is yours alone.
  - **badge** How a game works
    - **label** Win a rally
      - **cap** Your side scores a point.
    - **label** Reach the strike points target
      - **cap** Your side wins the game and rotates off.
    - **label** Or the organiser ejects a side
      - **cap** At any time. Points stand as scored.
    - **label** Ranking points by placement
      - **cap** Awarded by where you finished on your court that round. Score at least one point and you earn at least one.
  - **title** How the rounds run
    - **body** Each round mixes the whole pool into fresh courts, and within a court players cycle on and off as they win or lose. Only the side currently on court can score. When the round timer runs out, everyone is mixed again.
  - **title** Fair by design
    - **body** The queue always draws from the players with the least court time, so everybody plays about the same amount — and because partners keep changing, the standings reflect how you played, not who you were drawn with.


## Doghouse

  - **eyebrow** Queue mode
  - **h1** Doghouse
  - **lead** The same queue, with a crueller rule. The side in the doghouse scores side-outs to escape — and one lost rally sends the count back to zero.
  - **badge** How a game works
    - **label** Win a rally
      - **cap** Score a side-out.
    - **label** Lose a rally
      - **cap** Game lost — and the side-out count resets to zero.
    - **label** Reach your escape points target
      - **cap** Escaped. Back to the queue, with the win.
    - **label** Hit the loss limit
      - **cap** Ejected. The next players step in.
    - **label** Ranking points by placement
      - **cap** Score at least one side-out and you earn at least one ranking point.
  - **title** How the rounds run
    - **body** Each round mixes the whole pool into fresh courts, and within a court players cycle on and off. When the round timer runs out, everyone is mixed again.
  - **title** Fair by design
    - **body** The queue always draws from the players with the least court time, so everybody plays about the same amount.
